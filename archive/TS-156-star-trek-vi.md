# TS-156 — Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country (1991)

**Escape Hatch Episode:** 314  
**Air Date:** July 12, 2026  
**Topic:** Gene Roddenberry's life before Star Trek  
**True Story:** Deadheading on Pan Am flight that crashed in Syrian desert June 19, 1947; pulled Maharani of Phaltan from wreckage; 14 of 35 killed; local villagers stripped crash site  
**False Stories:** Amateur boxing career (11-3); CBS radio drama about first contact rejected as "too cerebral" (1949)

---

Star Trek VI gives us Gene Roddenberry's final chapter — a send-off for the original crew and for the man who created them. But before the Federation, before the Enterprise, before any of it, Roddenberry was just a young man from Los Angeles trying to figure out what he was made of.

For today's Truthsayer, three stories from Gene Roddenberry's life before Star Trek. Two are fiction. One is true.

Time for the Truthsayer.

**#1** — As a teenager in Los Angeles, Roddenberry had a brief amateur boxing career — eleven wins, three losses — before his mother threatened to stop feeding him if he took another punch.

**#2** — On June 19, 1947, Roddenberry was deadheading on a Pan Am flight and survived when it crashed in the Syrian desert.

**#3** — In 1949, Roddenberry wrote and submitted a radio drama to CBS about first contact between humans and an alien civilization. The network rejected it as "too cerebral for a general audience." He kept the rejection letter in his desk for the rest of his life.

Alright — which of these Gene Roddenberry stories is true?

It's #2.

Roddenberry was a Pan Am pilot deadheading through the Syrian desert, the plane goes down killing fourteen of the thirty-five people on board. He pulled the Maharani of Phaltan from the wreckage. By the time rescue arrived, local villagers had stripped the crash site of everything that remained. Well, I guess everything but his Tri-Corder.

Here ends the Truthsayer.
