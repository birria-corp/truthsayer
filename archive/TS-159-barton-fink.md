# TS-159 — Barton Fink (1991)

**Escape Hatch Episode:** 317  
**Air Date:** August 2, 2026  
**Topic:** Underwood typewriter pre-war promotions  
**True Story:** Underwood built world's largest typewriter at Atlantic City boardwalk; scrapped for war metal  
**False Stories:** Margaret Holloway speed tour (1926); Chrysler Building dispatch stunt (1930)

---

Barton Fink is a film about a man at war with a blank page — and the Underwood Universal typewriter sitting on his desk like a judge. That machine just stares at him. Waiting. The Underwood was no ordinary prop. By 1939, the company had produced five million machines. At the height of their industry, the Underwood factory in Hartford, CT turned out one typewriter every minute.

For today's Truthsayer, three stories about how Underwood promoted itself in the years before World War 2. Two are fiction. One is true.

Time for the Truthsayer.

**#1** — In 1926, Underwood sponsored a national typing speed tour, sending champion typist Margaret Holloway to 34 cities in 60 days — typing live in department store windows, drawing crowds of hundreds. Underwood advertised her as "the fastest fingers in America" and sold a record number of machines that Christmas.

**#2** — To celebrate the opening of the Chrysler Building in 1930, Underwood installed a fully functional Number 5 on the observation deck and invited journalists to type dispatches from the highest office in New York. The coverage ran in 47 cities.

**#3** — Underwood built the world's largest typewriter and put it on display at Garden Pier in Atlantic City for several years. It drew enormous crowds. Often, a young woman sat on each of the oversized keys.

Alright — which of these Underwood promotions actually happened?

It's #3.

Yeah, they built a 14 foot typewriter on the Atlantic City Boardwalk with Dames sitting on the keys. We used to be a real country.

Now I wonder if Joel and Ethan were using an Underwood when they typed "LOOK UPON ME! I'LL SHOW YOU THE LIFE OF THE MIND!"

Here ends the Truthsayer.
