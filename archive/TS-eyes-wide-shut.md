# TS — Eyes Wide Shut (1999)

**Escape Hatch Episode:** 309  
**Air Date:** May 28, 2026  
**Topic:** Sydney Pollack and fierce protection of his own work  
**True Story:** In 1991, Pollack sued Danish television after they screened Three Days of the Condor in pan-and-scan format. Court ruled it a mutilation of his work and violation of his Droit Moral — but ruled against him on a technicality anyway.  
**False Stories:** Built own home in Pacific Palisades without contractor; demanded Italian re-dub of The Firm at own expense after wrong voice actor used

---

Eyes Wide Shut gives us Sydney Pollack as Victor Ziegler — the most powerful and least trustworthy man in the room. It's an inspired piece of casting. Kubrick knew exactly what he was doing putting a legendary director in that chair.

For today's Truthsayer, three stories about Sydney Pollack and his fierce protection of his own work. Two are fiction. One is true.

Time for the Truthsayer.

**#1** — Pollack was a serious amateur architect who designed and built his own home in Pacific Palisades entirely without a contractor, doing much of the physical work himself over four years. He later said it was the only project he ever finished exactly as he intended.

**#2** — After completing The Firm in 1993, Pollack discovered the Italian distributor had re-dubbed Tom Cruise's dialogue with a voice actor who had previously dubbed Sylvester Stallone. He demanded a re-dub and the distributor complied.

**#3** — In 1991, Pollack sued Danish television after they screened Three Days of the Condor in pan-and-scan format. The court ruled the pan-scanning was a mutilation of his work and a violation of his Droit Moral — his legal right as an artist to protect the integrity of his work. He lost on a technicality anyway.

Alright — which of these Sydney Pollack stories is true?

It's #3.

Pollack took a foreign television network to court over an aspect ratio. And he nearly won. The court agreed the pan-scanning mutilated his film — then ruled against him on a technicality.

Hey, if you got that wrong, if you got that right. . . It happens all the time. Life goes on. It always does, until it doesn't. But you know that, don't you?

Here ends the Truthsayer.
